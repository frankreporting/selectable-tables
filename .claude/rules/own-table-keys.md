# The plugin's own table: keys, clicks and typing

Moved here from `CLAUDE.md` on 2026-11-26. This file loads by itself when a session opens a file under `paths`. When a change alters what it says, update it here.

**The keys on it (STA-80).** The navigation layer runs on the plugin's table with the rules it has on Obsidian's, and reads the table from the plugin, not the page:

- **Which table** comes from the plugin's list of the note's tables (`ownTables` in `layout.ts`, given by `own-table/index.ts`), whole, so the keys never see the seams between blocks: CodeMirror's `lineBlockAt` would give one block of 100 rows. Where the language copy isn't in place, a note's tables are Obsidian's, and the layer reads CodeMirror's blocks as before. The unit tests run every key test again with each table split into a block per row (`decide-blocks.test.ts`).
- **The selected cell** is drawn by the plugin's table (`markCell` in `model.ts`) whenever its row is drawn, so it stays outlined as its row leaves the page and comes back. Showing a cell scrolls the note, never the cell, since `scrollIntoView` would scroll the box that clips the table: a drawn row from its rect, sideways through `showSideways`, and a row not drawn, such as row 2,000 after Cmd+↓, by its estimated place first. The scroll's own measure then draws the rows there, and the cell is shown from where it's really drawn, before the frame is painted.
- **Rules only on the plugin's table** (`Layout.ownTables`): a tap of ↑ or ↓ beside a table stops on it and selects it, and the next goes on to the line beyond at the caret's place across, kept as the selection's `goalColumn`. A held key's repeats pass, read from the keydown by an `any` binding, and Vim's keys pass too. Enter on a selected cell opens it, in any table, since the plugin writes the cell's text itself. Obsidian keeps two guards for its own table only (read from 1.15.1: they look for its widget, and do nothing without one), so the plugin keeps them here: Backspace at the start of the line below selects the table, and an edit that leaves text right above a table puts a line break back at its start, in the same step (`blankLinesAbove`). Delete at the end of the blank line above does nothing, and Backspace at its start, under text, moves the caret up.
- **The caret rule:** a selection put inside a table's text by anything but a key or a press, such as closing Find, a Search pane result, Undo or another plugin's command, selects the table and the cell holding it (`caretInTable`), in a step of its own right after the update. Never during a composition or while Vim is driving.
- **A range of cells (STA-14):** Shift with an arrow grows a range from the selected cell, a drag across cells makes one (*Dragging across cells*, in `own-table-edits.md`), and Shift with a jump (Cmd+↑/↓/←/→, Home, End, Page Up and Down, Cmd+Home and End; Ctrl with Shift and an arrow on Windows and Linux) stretches it to the table's edge that way, along the row or column. `selectRange` in `cells.ts` keeps it as the cell it started from (`anchor`) and the cell moved to last (`selected`), which the note keeps in view, clear of what pins, and which Enter opens, Escape goes back to on its own, and Tab, the plain arrows and the commands start from: a plain arrow ends the range on its corner that way, its top left or its bottom right, as Obsidian's range does. A range of one cell is that cell selected, so the edit guard and typing below the table are the selected cell's, and Backspace, Delete, Cut and Paste act on it as on one cell (*Clearing, cutting and pasting cells*, in `own-table-cell-editing.md`). No move dispatches. The table draws it (`markCell` with an anchor in `model.ts`) with Obsidian's classes for its own range (item 16 of the list in `own-table.md`, `range.ts`), on the rows in the page as they're drawn, touching only rows whose part of the range changed, and `pins.ts` on the pinned copies of its cells; the outline of a selected cell doesn't show. Where anything pins, the frame sits inside its cells. Copy puts the range on the clipboard as Obsidian's range copies it (1.15.1's `copySelection`): the Markdown table as `text/plain` and `text/markdown`, every cell at its whole column's width (`copyRange`, through `readTable` and `writeTable`), with the freeze mark left out; Obsidian's `<!-- obsidian -->` marker then the Markdown rendered by `MarkdownRenderer` as `text/html`, the marker being what has Obsidian's own paste take the Markdown; and `obsidian/table`, each cell's text and each column's alignment, which a paste into Obsidian's table reads. Obsidian's table, the fallback, keeps Shift with an arrow doing nothing on a selected cell. *Own 110* to *Own 120* check it, each against the switch off where Obsidian has the same: the drawn classes against the range after each key, on the copies too, the frame in the window's picture, Copy's four forms against Obsidian's copy of the same range, a paste into a note, the keys on a range, Table 2000 by 12 stretched to its corner, and 60 seeded random steps; `window.__checkRangesOnly`, with `__checkOwnOnly` and `__checkCellsOnly`, runs only them.
- **Find:** Obsidian's Find marks its match with a mark decoration of class `obsidian-search-match-highlight`, from a field, and the Search pane its result with `is-flashing`; the table's blocks hide both, so `index.ts` reads them from the decorations facet on any transaction with effects, and the table draws them in their cells (`matches.ts`): on the matched words where the cell draws them as typed, else on the whole cell. Find's close puts the selection on the match, and a Search pane result a caret at its start, both with no user event, and the caret rule takes them.

Items 1 to 8, 11, 14, 16, 20 and 22 of the list in `obsidian-table.md`, and 9's clause on Obsidian's own moves between cells, hold for Obsidian's table only.

**Clicks (STA-112).** The plugin's table has two layers. A click on a cell selects it, and a drag selects cells; a double-click opens it, with the caret where it landed, and so does a click on the cell already selected alone, as in Numbers, which covers a slow double-click. In an open cell, a drag selects only its text. Obsidian's table, the fallback, keeps Obsidian's click. Preview builds had a setting, *Click to select a cell*, to open a cell with one click instead; it went before the release (BMT-137). The plugin keeps that way as a switch only the check script turns (`setClickSelects` on `debug`), so its other sections still check a click opening a cell.

- **The rule** is `clickOpens` in `drag-sums.ts`: a click opens a cell when the switch is off, when it's a double-click's second (`detail` 2 or more), on the open cell, or on the cell already selected alone. `clickCell` in `cell-drag.ts` reads it in the block's `click` listener in `model.ts`, after a drag has had its turn: a click that ended a range is swallowed before it.
- **A click that selects** enters as a drag does (`enter`): an open cell closes through `escapeCell`, the table is selected whole in one transaction, and `selectRange` with the anchor and head the same cell, without scrolling, so a double-click's second press lands on the same cell, and a pinned cell stays where it's pinned. A tap gives the note no focus, which would show a phone's keyboard; the tap that opens the cell focuses its editor inside its own handler, so iOS shows it then. So a tap's selection is a plain `select`: CodeMirror puts a `select.pointer` selection in the page even without focus, and that focuses the note. The block's `pointerdown` notes whether the press was a finger's, since WebKit's `click` may not say.
- **A drag in the open cell's text** stays its editor's, past the cell's edge. Let go over another cell, the browser sends its click to what the two share, a row or the table, so the block's `click` listener drops a click whose press began in the open cell and that ends outside it; taken for a click beside the cells, it selected the whole table (Brian's hand check, 11/24).
- **The pointer** over the plugin's cells is the arrow (`--cursor`) while a click selects, where Obsidian's `app.css` gives every table cell a text cursor, and a text cursor in the open cell's editor: `selectable-tables-click-selects` on the note's editor, from `editorAttributes` in `index.ts`, which CodeMirror reads on every update, so turning the switch calls `workspace.updateOptions()`.
- **A press keeps the selected cell** for the table to decide: `onLeave` in `cells.ts` takes the cell off at any press in the note, before anything else sees it, except, on the plugin's table, a press on a cell of the same table. Without that, a click could never find the cell already selected.
- **Four quick clicks** still select every cell: the first selects the cell, the second opens it, the third selects a line in its editor (CodeMirror reads `detail`), and the fourth goes to `selectEvery`.
- **How the check script notices:** *Own 157* to *Own 166*, the test plan's steps, with real presses, double-clicks and keys through the window's debugger and taps through `Input.synthesizeTapGesture` under a phone's layout: a click selects Basil, a double-click opens Balcony with the caret where it landed, a second click on Kitchen opens it, a drag in the open Needs sun stays text past the table's edge, a click elsewhere closes it, drags, Shift+click, right-click, four clicks and links as before, a pinned header cell deep in *Frozen table 2000 by 12* and Plant 4 in the frozen strip opened in place, no settings tab and the switch off, and a click's own work timed against opening. Every other section of the plugin's table runs with the switch off, so they still check a click opening a cell. `window.__checkClicksOnly`, with `__checkOwnOnly` and `__checkCellsOnly`, runs only them.

**Typing over a cell (STA-13).** A key that types on a selected cell of the plugin's table, or on a range's cell moved to last, replaces the cell's text, as a spreadsheet does (Brian, 11/24); Enter still opens it with the caret at the end. `typeOn` in `decide.ts` picks it before `typeBelow`, which a table selected whole, and Obsidian's table, keep.

- **In the key's own event.** The keydown opens the cell (`typeOver` in `extension.ts`, through the table's `typeOver`) and leaves the key's default alone, so the browser types the key in the cell's editor, which has the keyboard by then. Text with no keydown, the emoji picker's or dictation's, goes in through `beforeinput`.
- **An accent key or an input method's first key** (`composes` in `decide.ts`: key code 229, or `Dead`, `Process`, `Unidentified`) opens the cell emptied and is lost: the note lets the keyboard go during it, and the cell's editor takes it once the key is up (`handOver`). On a Mac the text box has to hold the keyboard before such a key: moved to the cell's editor during it, macOS dropped the accent half made (Option+E then E gave `´e`), and the editor went on composing for good, so Escape stopped working (Brian's hand check, 11/25). `Input.imeSetComposition` skips macOS's input handling, so the check script can't see this; only real keys can. Composing in the note over the selected table is never allowed (*Never interfere with a running composition*). BMT-134 holds typing them straight into the cell, through a hidden text box as Google Sheets has it.
- **Emptied in the cell's editor** (`typeOver` in `open-cell.ts`), not with its text selected: auto-pair would wrap the old text in `[` or `*`, and a header cell's freeze mark would go with it. The mark stays, with a space after it. The caret goes to the old text's end first, which puts the note's selection there, so Undo puts it back in the cell; the emptying carries `isolateHistory.of('before')`, and the typing, right after it and touching it, joins its step in the note's history (Obsidian's `newGroupDelay` is 500 ms; a composition always joins). Undo with the cell closed leaves the caret in its text, and the caret rule selects the cell.
- **The selected cell's row stays drawn** while its block is in the page (`keptRow` in `model.ts`), as a pinned cell's about to open does, so the editor can exist in the keydown: a pinned row's real row sits at the table's top, in the first block, which CodeMirror keeps in the page while the table is selected, since the note's selection starts there. Far from a cell whose block left the page, the key would land on the selected table, so the note lets the keyboard go (`blur`), the cell opens emptied once it's drawn, and the key is lost.
- **How the check script notices:** *Own 175* to *Own 185*, the test plan's steps with real keys through the window's debugger, a dead key and a Japanese composition simulated and finished with `Input.insertText`, each opening the cell and lost, then working in the open cell with Escape after, the emoji picker's text by `Input.insertText` alone, a far cell with a key and a composition, and a key's round trip against Enter's on Table 2000 by 12. *Own 39* and *Own 115* now check that typing replaces a selected cell and a range's cell. `window.__checkTypingOnly`, with `__checkOwnOnly` and `__checkCellsOnly`, runs only them.
